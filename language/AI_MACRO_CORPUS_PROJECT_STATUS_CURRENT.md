## 5. Current batching policy for subsequent domains

The working default is now:

**15 → 10 → 5 → optional 5 if needed**

This is a research-control sequence, **not a source quota**.

- **First 15:** establish broad conceptual foundation and a strong novelty baseline.
- **Next 10:** deliberately close remaining evidence/language gaps and test provisional observations.
- **Next 5:** close the foundation or, if the foundation is already complete, operate as a saturation challenge.
- **Optional final 5:** broad post-foundation challenge only when needed to confirm saturation or when a conceptual gap reopens.

Completion remains based on **coverage + saturation + auditability**, not on reaching 30 or 35 papers.

For novelty/saturation judgments, only genuinely new generalizable analytical machinery counts. Topic-specific vocabulary and reinforcement of already learned grammar do not count as universal novelty.

## 6. Cross-domain learning architecture that remains in force

The project is learning three distinct layers:

1. **Universal analytical grammar**  
   Evidence ordering, observation versus inference, causal calibration, null precision, heterogeneity, magnitude, mechanism separation, limitations, quantitative economy, synthesis, sentence/paragraph architecture, etc.

2. **Domain-conditioned language**  
   The nouns, verbs, relationships, comparisons, mechanisms and stylistic structures characteristic of each AI Macro domain.

3. **Evidence-conditioned expression**  
   Predicate strength and sentence structure conditioned on evidence type, identification strength, horizon, subgroup, boundary, proxy quality and validation stage.

The working conceptual model remains:

> **universal analytical grammar × domain-conditioned language × evidence-conditioned expression = appropriate analytical prose**

No final framework is to be legislated until all eleven domains are complete.

## 8. Current Finance universal candidates

Finance has added or promoted nine general analytical candidates so far:

1. **Level versus change/intervention** — the level of a variable and a change imposed on it are separate evidentiary objects.
2. **Baseline state versus shock sensitivity** — a group can begin worse without responding more strongly to a shock.
3. **Decision-time information set** — evaluate decisions with information actually available at the decision point, not hindsight.
4. **Transition effect versus enduring regime effect** — implementation/transition consequences and persistent steady-state effects are different claims.
5. **Balance-sheet stock versus transaction flow** — outstanding stock, issuance, refinancing, repayment and committed-facility drawdown are non-equivalent.
6. **Two-sided financing outcome decomposition** — observed price/quantity/maturity/channel usage can jointly reflect borrower demand and lender/supplier capacity.
7. **Upstream commitment/booked state versus downstream realization** — orders, commitments and backlog are not realized output.
8. **Current state versus adjustment velocity** — level and speed/path of adjustment are separate analytical objects.
9. **Contractual trigger changes control architecture** — a covenant/rule threshold can reallocate control rights before full economic failure/default.

These remain cross-domain **candidates**, not final universal rules.
